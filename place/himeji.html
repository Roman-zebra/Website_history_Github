Himeji Castle

姫路城

Compare on the map

What to look for

Find Himeji Castle and follow the main approach south into the city. Compare the castle grounds, green spaces and the arrangement of streets around them.

Himeji Castle was completed in 1609 and has never been destroyed by war, fire or earthquake.

Air raids in June and July 1945 left the city centre around it in ashes. The castle came through and is still the original wooden building.

Its white walls give it the nickname the White Heron Castle.

The old photo here is from the 1960s, while the city was still being rebuilt. Slide across and watch the streets fill in.

Why open this place

Himeji Castle is known for coming through the war. Two large air raids in June and July 1945 left the city centre in ashes, and the castle survived.

The castle has changed in other ways. In the Showa restoration of 1956–1964 the main keep was taken down and put back together, and the 1960s photographs come from around the end of that work.

On the map, step by step

  • The inner moat. Its curve to the north and west of the keep is the same in both photographs, which makes it the easiest thing to line up.
  • Sannomaru Square, the open lawn just south of the keep. In the 1960s photograph the same ground is covered with rows of large buildings.
  • The keep itself. A temporary cover building was put over it at the start of the restoration, and work on the keep complex finished at the end of March 1964. Depending on when the frame was taken, the old photograph may show repair work, so zoom in and look.

Reading the photographs

1961–1969 is the date range of the GSI series. The map does not show the date of this particular frame; GSI’s Map and Aerial Photo Viewing Service can help you find it.

The old photographs stop at zoom level 17.

Visiting

The castle is open from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., with last entry at 4 p.m., and closed on 29 and 30 December. Check the official site before you go, as arrangements can change.

The stairs inside the keep and turrets are very steep and narrow. There are handrails but no lifts, and the official site says wheelchair users cannot make the visit on their own.

The main keep is thought to have been largely complete by the end of 1609, under Ikeda Terumasa.
