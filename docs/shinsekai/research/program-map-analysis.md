# Contemporary Luna Park program: museum preview and topology

The [Osaka Museum of History's 2012 exhibition page](https://www.osakamushis.jp/news/2012/tenjigae/120523.html) publishes a **350 × 252 pixel preview** of the reverse side of a Taisho-period `ルナパークプログラム` with a bird's-eye map. The museum owns the copy. This is likely the same kind of original program listed by Osaka Prefectural Library as `枚-282`, but object identity has **not** been established. The image's date within the Taisho period and reuse permission are unspecified. The museum page is copyrighted; study its geometry and factual description only. No museum image was copied into Git or the product. Ledger S029.

The preview confirms a bird's-eye drawing with the first tower at the park entrance/edge, a second upright White Tower farther inside, a connecting ropeway, and numerous built attractions. The museum's own description names **Circling Wave**, theatres and film houses as dense components of the park. At 350 pixels across, facility labels, scale and building footprints cannot be read reliably. The drawing is pictorial, not a survey. Do not digitize its pixels as metre coordinates.

The same page describes a separate three-panel period postcard: from its White Tower view, the **music hall is in front of White Tower**, the **Circling Wave is to its left in the image**, and the **Mystery Hall farther along**. Claude's review of the small image found its left/right order consistent with the museum caption; a tower-side, northeast-to-southwest camera is plausible. The 1913-album description refers to a different image and framing, so its left/right labels should remain separate until the original photographs can be compared at useful resolution. Ledger S030.

The page corrects our earlier search conclusion: an **online preview of a genuine program drawing does exist**, but no sufficiently sharp original scan for tracing was found. This resolves some topology for T2 while leaving exact facility coordinates, plan date and 1912 opening-day configuration unresolved. A library visit or authorized high-resolution copy would improve the eventual park reconstruction; tower exterior work can rely on the separate 1912/1914 photographic controls in the meantime.

## Separate Edo-Tokyo Museum guide-map postcard

The [Edo-Tokyo Museum digital archive](https://www.edohakuarchives.jp/detail-106762.html) displays an original **新世界案内図** postcard, collection `88133639`, from the Kitagawa Chikashi collection. Its catalogue dates it only to the **Taisho period** (1912–1926); it does not establish a 1912 opening-day state, scale, north direction, or relationship to the 1913 developer album. In the enlarged browser view, the park block, a central feature labelled `池`, surrounding plots, and the neighbouring radial street blocks are visible. Tiny attraction/plot labels need independent reading before use. This is a period diagram, not a survey fit control.

[Japan Search's object record](https://jpsearch.go.jp/item/tokyomuseumcolection-edo_tokyo_museumjbD88133639) says image use generally requires permission and marks copyright. We did not copy the image into the repository or product. Ledger S073. Claude has been asked to compare its readable labels and topology with the separate 1913 album and museum program, preserving each object's date and rights.

Claude's [visual transcription](edohaku-guide-map-claude.md) identifies a north arrow pointing to the right and tentative legend entries for the first tower, White Tower, music hall, park gate, various halls and an `角力常設館敷地` (permanent sumo-hall site). Several tiny labels are uncertain; Codex has not independently read every character. Claude could not directly compare the unavailable full 1913 album plan, so that comparison remains open. The postcard's pond is unlabelled by name and cannot settle whether the sources' `真澄が池` and `夫婦池` are the same water feature.

The site legend provides a **possible** date clue, not a date of 1918–1919. [Osaka City's local history](https://www.city.osaka.lg.jp/naniwa/page/0000000915.html) dates the completed Shinsekai Osaka Kokugikan to **September 1919**, but no source yet establishes when its site was first reserved or when this postcard was printed. A printed guide could also persist after construction. Keep the museum's broad Taisho-period catalogue date, and do not promote the pictured facility list to the July 1912 scene without earlier corroboration.

## 1913 album in Osaka Museums' online catalogue

The [Digital Osaka Museums collection search](https://dom.ocm.osaka/database/search) returns **192 catalogue records** for `大阪新名所新世界`, including a [Luna Park panorama](https://dom.ocm.osaka/database/osakamushis/28134) and a [fountain-bath floor plan](https://dom.ocm.osaka/database/osakamushis/28188). The inspected panorama page displays ownership/group metadata but no viewable scan. A separate search for `新世界 平面図` returned only the fountain-bath plan, not a full district/site plan. This is a search-index observation, **not proof that the original album lacked a district plan**. The [Edo-Tokyo Museum exhibition list](https://www.edo-tokyo-museum.or.jp/assets/img/2015/03/list.pdf) dates the Osaka Museum of History's copy of the developer's album to **20 June 1913**. Treat these catalogue titles as source leads; no museum image or original page text is licensed or adopted for the product. Ledger S074.
