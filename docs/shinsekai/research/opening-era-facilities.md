# Opening-era facility evidence

The [Osaka Prefectural Library's exhibition handout](https://www.library.pref.osaka.jp/contents/wp-content/uploads/66_runa.pdf) identifies a 1912 printed item, *大坂名勝* (大村書店, call number M291-72N), and describes its displayed images. This anchors several features closer to opening than the 1913 developer album or 1914 guide. It does **not** give an exposure date for each illustration or prove operation on the exact opening day, 1912-07-03. Ledger S018.

| Feature | What the library identifies from the 1912 item | What remains unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 埃及館 and 不思議館 | Both Luna Park attractions; 不思議館 presented a mechanical stage scene. | Floor plans, engineering and exact opening date. No historical person/character likeness will be reconstructed. |
| 美人探検館 | Combined stage performance and projected images. | Performers, program and equipment design. The planned empty-stage effects are an inference. |
| White Tower | Lookout, ropeway link to first tower, fountain and rest area at its foot. | Terminal geometry and exact fountain placement. |
| First tower arch interior | Decorated ceiling. | Artist, motif and colours. Generic ornament should be labelled as inferred. |

The [library's reference answer](https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/reference/show?id=1000291784) cites the 1913 developer album: the roof garden was about 50 shaku above ground, with an elevator from there to the tower top. The [library timeline](https://www.library.pref.osaka.jp/contents/wp-content/uploads/66_runa.pdf) says the elevator connection from ground to observation deck came in a **1938** renovation. Therefore a ground-floor elevator boarding animation in the 1912 mode would be an anachronism. Ledger S019.

The same library timeline dates second-stage development and the radium bath to **1913**, and a 1920 commercial sign to **1920**. Those must be absent from the 1912 mode. The 1913 album's table of contents names many further halls and rides, but its publication date is not proof of their presence on opening day. Confirm them against the original program before T5/T6.

The [1914 *大阪独案内*, NDL canvas 95](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/952032/1/95) describes a park of **“四千有余坪”**, a little over 4,000 tsubo (roughly 13,200 m²). Its White Tower stood on an artificial mound near the park centre; water from a large fall at its foot reached a pond named **夫婦池**, with a Japanese-style garden nearby. The text also names 埃及館, 不思議館, 清華殿 and 動物館 around the park. These are **1914 conditions** until earlier sources establish their 1912 opening date. The number printed for White Tower height is difficult to read in the available scan and is deliberately not used as a model dimension. Do not silently equate 夫婦池 with the library-captioned 真澄が池: the sources may use different names or refer to different water features.

[Osaka Prefectural Library's ropeway reference answer](https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/reference/show?id=1000254008), citing Saito's 1985 technical history, independently reports a **45 m white-painted White Tower**, an approximately **100 m double-track shuttle** with **two four-seat cabins**, and boarding from the **first tower's base-building roof** to a White Tower-side station at approximately the same elevation. Claude separately inspected the underlying book and a 1934 retrospective, which gives White Tower as **150 shaku** (about 45 m) and places the ropeway from the first tower's roof garden. The unclear 1914 height should therefore not be read as 130 shaku. The White Tower's **top** is distinct from its ropeway **boarding level**. These are source-level dimensions, not coordinates; the stations and tower footprint still need map/photo alignment. Ledger S038–S040.

A closer visual check is [*南海の栞* (published 1912), printed pp.40–45](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NDL946866_%E5%8D%97%E6%B5%B7%E3%81%AE%E6%A0%9E.pdf?page=28): its district and park-to-tower photographs appear on PDF page 28, a White Tower/park view and text on page 29, and a night image on page 30. The park-side tower view confirms a row of buildings between the camera and the tower already in the 1912 publication. The White Tower view shows a prominent curved/oval foreground feature, but its identity and exact footprint cannot yet be fixed from the photograph alone. See `photo-controls.md` and ledger S028. The book's publication year is not an individual photo exposure date.

Claude's C2 reading of the same 1912 volume identifies text on printed p.44 describing a **fountain pond and music hall before White Tower** and **five-colour illumination reflected in a waterfall**. Its p.42 photograph appears to show a peaked-roof octagonal music hall on a circular/terraced foreground, distinct in form from the open-column pavilion in the northern garden photograph. These features may be assigned to the **1912 published scene**, although publication date alone does not prove the exact opening-day operating state. The 1914 name `夫婦池` and the library's `真澄が池` are still not equated.

The [Osaka Museum of History's 2012 exhibition page](https://www.osakamushis.jp/news/2012/tenjigae/120523.html) provides a small original program drawing and a more useful written account of a period three-panel postcard: music hall **in front of White Tower**, Circling Wave **to the image's left**, and Mystery Hall farther along. The program also depicts the tower-to-White-Tower ropeway and a dense ring of rides, theatres and film houses. Its exact date is only “Taisho period,” so this is park-era topology, **not a confirmed 1912-07-03 arrangement**. See `program-map-analysis.md` and ledger S029–S030.
