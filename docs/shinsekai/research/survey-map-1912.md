# 1912 measured Osaka map as a street and parcel control

The original *實測大阪地図* sheet 3-8-甲, published by 昇竜堂 in 1912, is available as an individually CC0-marked original from [Osaka Municipal Library](https://image.oml.city.osaka.lg.jp/da/detail?tilcod=0000000021-OSK0186828) (ledger S063). The adjoining 2-8-乙 [sheet](https://image.oml.city.osaka.lg.jp/da/detail?tilcod=0000000021-OSK0186821) is a Tennoji-side control (S064). The original [index and symbol legend](https://image.oml.city.osaka.lg.jp/da/detail?tilcod=0000000021-OSK0186791) is separately CC0-marked (S069). All three are under `assets-src/shinsekai/references/oml/maps/`; Claude's first-pass reading is archived as `map1912-claude.md`.

On direct visual inspection of 3-8-甲, the radial streets, semicircular frontage, parcels and their labels, tram corridor, and southern railway corridor are discernible. This is a land/road survey: it does **not** show which parcel held each Luna Park attraction or building. The dashed circle immediately south of the semicircular frontage is compatible with the expected first-tower area. The index's key does **not** assign a separate symbol to dashed circles. A 1912 description calls the tower base roughly square (`方十数間`), so a round pond or planned feature is also plausible. The circle's identity and relation to the tower remain **unresolved**. The key also lacks a marked elevation benchmark, so this map does not settle whether a later 250-shaku figure is sea elevation or building height. The sheets must first be oriented and scaled using the printed map scale and several persistent street/rail intersections; one visually plausible circle is insufficient for a geographic coordinate.

Next alignment work:

1. Use the now-inspected index to confirm sheet boundaries and distinguish boundary dashes from a feature outline; seek a separate plan or photograph to identify the circle. Do not label it the tower or a pond yet.
2. Choose at least four surviving intersections or rail/street controls distributed around Shinsekai, and tie both sheets to the 1928 and 1942 GSI aerial layers and modern coordinates. Record source pixels, target coordinates, transform method and residual errors.
3. Compare the possible tower circle with the 1913 plan topology, the 1914 and CC0 north photographs, and the documented roughly 30 m displacement from the modern second tower. Only then replace `layout-1912.geojson`'s approximate first-tower point.
4. Continue seeking the original 1913 site/facility plan for actual attraction footprints. Parcel boundaries alone cannot supply those.

The map date is a publication year, not proof that every road or planned tram depot shown was complete on the opening day. The term `大阪市電鉄車庫予定地` explicitly describes a *planned* site. Do not promote it to a built 1912 feature.

## Registration check in progress (2026-09-30)

The research cache now has fixed GSI `ort_1928` and `ort_riku10` z18 aerial tiles and a matching current `std` street-map mosaic, fetched by `scripts/fetch-shinsekai-gsi.cjs`. Seven-by-seven tile mosaics include the southern railway corridor and more outer road crossings. These layers are **different dates** and are used only to select surviving road and rail controls; the current map cannot prove a feature's 1912 existence. A first crop was inadvertently displayed with a quarter-turn from the source sheet. Returning the crop to the sheet's original page orientation puts the tram corridor west of the semicircular frontage, consistent with the later aerials. The circle and the modern garden may occupy different places: without a fitted transformation, neither is a geographic control or a tower position. The scale caption printed right-to-left reads `縮尺壹千` (1:1000), and the bar is numbered 0–100, but the bar's distance unit is not printed clearly. Do not convert map pixels to metres from the bar until its unit or the physical scan scale is independently established.

The 1912 central street drawing does not line up by an obvious rigid shift and rotation with the 1928 or 1936–1942 photographed grid. Trial SIFT and ORB matches on the original sheet and later mosaics concentrated on repeated blocks and gave degenerate transforms; a constrained voting trial found no robust cluster. **No georeference or feature coordinate follows from these trials.** The 1912 sheet's publication date alone cannot establish whether every road line represents an as-built condition. Check a contemporaneous plan against later aerials and then manually select four distributed road/rail controls that demonstrably persist. Record residuals and investigate any nonuniform local discrepancy before using the map for geometry.
