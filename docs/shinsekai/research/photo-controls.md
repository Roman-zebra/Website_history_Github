# Near-opening photographic controls for the first tower

The opening-era three-view set is **provisional**. It now includes a photograph in a book **published in 1912**, plus views in two books published in 1914. Publication establishes an upper bound on exposure date, not that a feature existed on 1912-07-03. Use the views to build and compare the tower; recheck any element that could have changed after opening. Sources are in `ledger.csv` (S003, S017, S028). The 1914 NDL inspection scans remain in `../research-cache/ndl/`, outside Git; the 1912 pages were examined in Chrome from Commons' copy of the NDL scan.

## Rights-cleared three-direction alternatives

The Osaka Municipal Library has published individual **CC0** original-object scans, verified in Chrome on 2026-09-30 and saved under `assets-src/shinsekai/references/oml/` (ledger S031–S037). [Tower frontage `c0234001`](https://image.oml.city.osaka.lg.jp/da/detail?tilcod=0000000021-OSK0157352) shows the arch with an octagonal pavilion in the foreground, but **its camera side is unresolved**: the pavilion resembles the park's music hall south of the tower, while a similar pavilion appears in the confirmed northern 1914 view A. [White Tower viewpoint `d0285001`](https://image.oml.city.osaka.lg.jp/da/detail?tilcod=0000000021-OSK0158887) looks **north** from the **south** over the park toward the arch. [1912 Ebisudori `c1819001`](https://image.oml.city.osaka.lg.jp/da/detail?tilcod=0000000021-OSK0158514) follows the **northwest radial** toward the tower. The latter two are distinct CC0 camera sectors; `c0234001` cannot yet serve as a confirmed third direction. Exact camera stations and lenses remain unknown. The first two records are dated only `1912–1925`, so match stable structure, not an opening-day claim. The Ebisudori record says `1912`, although the day is unspecified.

The [White Tower and ropeway `d0289001`](https://image.oml.city.osaka.lg.jp/da/detail?tilcod=0000000021-OSK0158891) and [tower-and-pavilion panorama `e0347001`](https://image.oml.city.osaka.lg.jp/da/detail?tilcod=0000000021-OSK0160200) are complementary rights-cleared composition controls. The panorama's camera side is likewise unresolved. The [1912 opening commemorative colour triptych `e0343001`](https://image.oml.city.osaka.lg.jp/da/detail?tilcod=0000000021-OSK0160196) names Mystery Hall, White Tower and music hall but its decorative printing cannot establish the actual 1912 colour scheme.

For the **north-facing blockout**, use 1914 view A for the early roofline and 1921 plate 46 for larger structural detail. View A's park entrance sign visible through the arch and the ropeway cabin on image right support a northern camera. The 1921 name band may be a later addition; do not transfer it uncritically to 1912. Compare `c0234001` and `e0347001` only for structures that are independent of facing until their pavilion and arch details can settle direction. This keeps the three-view reconstruction gate open rather than treating an unproved camera sector as complete. Claude's review is preserved in `tower-study-review-claude.md`.

The [1914 *大阪独案内* canvas 95 left photo](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/952032/1/95) remains a corroborating elevated view. Its southern camera position is plausible, but the photo has no station caption. *南海の栞* printed pp.40–45 also gives a district panorama (p.40), a park-to-tower view (p.41), White Tower/park view (p.42), explanatory text (p.43–44) and a very dark night image (p.45). The top of its p.42 photograph shows White Tower beyond an oval park feature; it can anchor broad park composition, not measured coordinates. The 1912 volume's NDL IIIF manifest returned 404 and NDL Lab OCR returned 403; no repeated API calls are planned. Commons' file page labels the PDF a public-domain scan but also notes a 2019 Japanese administrative adjudication; confirm rights for an individual image before product reuse.

Claude's C2 comparison now places the 1912 p.41 park-to-tower camera at a **southern high point, plausibly White Tower, looking north to north-northeast**. The p.42 White Tower photo appears to look southwest from the first tower. The museum's small program bird's-eye preview similarly puts White Tower on the first tower's southwest side. These are directional checks, not surveyed camera positions. Claude also corrected its 1914 Ebisudori view to **northwest → southeast**, agreeing with Osaka City's radial-street description. The complete raw handoff is `photo-directions-claude.md`.

Two [Commons photos from Jan-Jan Alley](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Original_Tsutenkaku_from_Jan-Jan_Alley.jpg) and [beside Pavilion 1](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Original_Tsutenkaku_and_Ichigo-kan.jpg) were inspected in Chrome as **supplemental south-side views**. Commons lists both as public domain in Japan and the US but dates them only 1912–1920 and gives a now-unverified local-history source. The first photo shows the tower mostly behind later-looking buildings and advertising; the second shows a theatre facade and lower ironwork, not the full tower. Neither should substitute for a dated 1912 elevation or copy the advertisements into the scene. Ledger S026–S027.

The [Osaka Prefectural Library exhibition](https://www.library.pref.osaka.jp/nakato/shotenji/66_runa.html) names an octagonal music hall in a view *from* White Tower toward the first tower, but calls the direction south. The park sits south of the first tower, so the White Tower-to-first-tower sightline is northward; the library's direction word appears erroneous. This discrepancy does not establish that A's foreground structure is the same music hall. Keep that feature's coordinates unset pending the original 1913 album/program map.

No modern reproduction, video frame, or photograph is embedded in the product at this stage. Before using a historical photograph in the final site, retain its individual source, rights terms and attribution, and remove protected signs or people from any derived 3D asset.
