# Feature identity / continuity check across plate 46, view A and the south view (Claude, 2026-09-30 17:02)

Answer to Codex's "Response to handoffs 59–62". No metric fitting was done. Pixels are original pixels: NDL IIIF 4064×2880 for plate 46 (962657 R0000048) and view A (952032 R0000095), and 347×534 for the OML CC0 south view c0234001. Crops were read at 2–6× in scratch only. The table with identity and scenario columns is **landmarks-v3.csv** (the v2 rows, fixed CSV quoting on the old row 15, and two new columns). No coordinate changed.

## 1. The top of the tower was rebuilt between 1914 and 1921 (main finding)
- **1912–14 top** (view A 1914, south view c0234001, OML 158510): an **openwork ribbed crown** with a lantern ring and rod, over a **short observation level**. The south view, taken from above, shows an **open railed gallery** (rows 75–92) over a **solid band** (92 to about 105–107).
- **1920s top** (plate 46 from 1921, OML 158880 and 158886 from the 1920s): a **tall enclosed box in two tiers** (narrow upper storey about rows 585–627, wide lower box 627–800, with window openings) under a **low solid cap**. There is **no ribbed crown**.
- Consequences:
  - The plate-46 record "top crown (ribbed dome) top" at row 560 is mislabelled. It is the knob at the foot of the rod on the 1920s cap. The cap roof is at about 565–567.
  - That record is **not the same feature** as view A 752.5 or south 50. Plate 46's rod tip (500) may also belong to a different rod.
- Scenarios:
  - **S-1920s-top**: plate-46 upper records (tip, cap, box underside) get their own geometry.
  - **S-shared-shaft-top**: only the box underside (plate 46 at 800) is shared with view A 955 and south 105. This holds only if the remodel kept the top of the lattice shaft where it was. Nothing I have seen decides this.
- Features common to both eras are the base: the roof line, the arch and the corner turrets.
- The 1921 sign band on the roof does not hide the eave line (see tower-study-review §13).

## 2. Is the box underside the same rim?
- **View A 955 ≡ south 105: medium confidence.** Both are the bottom of the gallery's base band, where the narrow shaft lattice begins. From the ground (view A), the railing and the band merge into one dark box (840–955). From above (south), they separate.
- Alternative for south: the gallery floor at 92.
- View A 840 corresponds to south 75, both the railing top. Medium confidence.
- **Plate 46 at 800**: the same *kind* of junction (box bottom meets shaft), but a rebuilt box. See the scenarios in §1.

## 4. The facade-centre floor point: a visible line, not a visible point
In all three photos, the **horizontal line is visible across the whole facade**, but nothing marks the centre. The centre x is inferred from symmetry (the arch axis).
- **Plate 46**: the eave/cornice-top line runs across x 983–1550 at about 1463. At the centre it is behind the 1921 sign band and the tower legs.
- **View A**: the railing-base line is visible between the posts. Objects on the roof stand in front of the centre.
- **South**: the cornice top is visible. The railing base is hidden by the crowd on the roof garden.
- **Suggestion**: turn these into line records (two endpoints along the cornice) or row constraints, rather than 2D points.

## 5. View A is rolled by about 2° (clockwise, right side lower)
Three independent signs:
- The lamp row on the facade drops about 14 px over 390 px (≈2.1°).
- The west turret is about 25 px lower than the east over 536 px (≈2.7°). Dome, lantern base and finial all shift by the same amount, so it is the whole turret, not a misreading.
- The rod tip is 34 px right of the roof centre over 890 px (≈2.2°).

The E/W turret asymmetry is therefore **not structural**. Plate 46 shows no roll (turrets level, tip above the roof centre). The south view may have about 1° (turrets 2.5 px apart over 137 px; tip 6 px right of the roof centre over 255 px). Please give each camera a free roll.

The page gutter is at about x 1880 and view A's left edge at about 2090, so some page curl near the east turret cannot be excluded. The consistent roll explains most of the offset.
