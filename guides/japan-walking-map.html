Japan Walking Map: Hidden Gems, Small Towns & Historic Streets

Open the map

A memorable walk in Japan can start with a very small question: where did this road lead, what stood beside this station, or why does the shoreline look different in an old photograph? Japan Time Atlas puts historical aerial imagery beside the modern map so you can choose places to investigate on foot.

Build a walk around one neighbourhood

Choose an anchor such as a station, temple or waterfront. Open its location on the map, compare the surrounding street pattern, then save a few nearby points that interest you. Check the real walking route, crossings and current access before setting out. Aerial imagery helps explain a place; it is not a turn-by-turn walking route.

Where to start exploring

  • Tokyo — Asakusa: compare a familiar sightseeing district with its earlier street pattern, then look at the smaller streets around it.
  • Kanazawa: start with the city's history and look for details in the older streets.
  • Yokohama — Minato Mirai: compare the waterfront's industrial past with its present-day shape.
  • Shime, near Fukuoka: explore how an industrial landmark sits within a residential landscape.

A different kind of Japan itinerary

Use the map to connect famous sights with ordinary surroundings rather than trying to visit every pin. For a day trip, choose one area and leave time for the walk itself. You can search in English, Japanese, Korean and both Chinese scripts. Saved places stay in the browser where you saved them; keep important travel details separately if you will change devices.

Photography and respectful street walks

Older shopfronts, waterways and railway traces can be interesting even when they are not major attractions. Streets are also people's homes and workplaces. Follow signs, avoid blocking narrow paths and ask before photographing people closely. Confirm transport, opening hours and any required reservations with the relevant operator.
