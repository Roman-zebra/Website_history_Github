Japan Historical Maps & Old Aerial Photos: Then and Now

Open the map

Historical maps and old aerial photographs reveal different sides of a city. A map names streets and landmarks; a photograph shows the landscape at the time of the survey. Japan Time Atlas brings historical aerial imagery and a modern map together, helping you explore the same place across time.

How to compare old and new Japan

  1. Open a featured place or find a location using the map search.
  2. Select the historical comparison when imagery is available.
  3. Read the displayed survey year and move the slider across roads, water and blocks of buildings.
  4. Save a point you would like to investigate on a later walk.

Coverage and dates vary by area. Some places have imagery close to the end of the Second World War; others use different periods. Missing imagery does not mean a place did not exist. The map is an exploration tool, not a complete archive of every year.

Three ways to read a changing landscape

  • Waterfronts: compare Yokohama and Kobe to look for changes around harbours and reclaimed land.
  • City streets: explore Tokyo and Hiroshima, paying attention to road alignments and open spaces.
  • Landmarks and their surroundings: compare Himeji with the streets around its castle, or inspect a railway station and nearby blocks.

For architecture, geography and photography

You do not need an upcoming trip to use the atlas. It can prompt questions about urban development, railway history and the everyday buildings that survive around famous sights. When taking a new street photograph, note your date and viewpoint so that later comparisons have context. Differences in camera angle, shadows and image resolution can make buildings look different without proving that they changed.

Sources and interpretation

Historical aerial imagery is provided through Geospatial Information Authority of Japan tiles. Place information also uses OpenStreetMap and Wikipedia, with attribution on the site. Consult GSI's tile catalogue and the site's source and licence notes when you need the original material or wish to reuse it. Confirm specific historical claims with the cited source rather than inferring them from imagery alone.
