THE ATLAS · 01 — 05

Explore Japan on foot, across time

Japan Time Atlas is a free map for curious walks through Japan. Compare historical aerial photographs with the present-day map, then explore castles, neighbourhood streets, small local sights and places with a liminal atmosphere.

Plan a walk beyond the main sights

Start with Tokyo, Kyoto or Osaka, then look closely at the surrounding streets. A station, a small shrine, a waterfront or the remains of an old railway can become the starting point for a different kind of Japan itinerary. Save interesting places in this browser and open them again while planning your walk.

Old maps, street photography and everyday history

Use the comparison slider to study how coastlines, street patterns and neighbourhoods have changed. This is also a way to explore Japanese architecture and urban history from home. Historical photo coverage and survey years vary by location; use the year shown on the map rather than assuming every photograph was taken in 1945.

Liminal Japan and retro places

Discover unusual stations, former industrial sites and quiet spaces where the past feels close. Liminal is an atmosphere, not a promise that a place is empty or abandoned. Check official opening and access information before visiting, and explore only public or permitted areas.

Featured places

Compare historical aerial photographs with today. Explore Japan’s castles, temples, stations and local places in English, Japanese, Korean, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese.

Hiroshima

Trace the Motoyasu River beside the Atomic Bomb Dome, then compare the surrounding blocks with Peace Memorial Park. The dome is one of the surviving atomic-bombed structures, not the only building that remained.

Tokyo — Asakusa

Find Senso-ji and follow the approach towards the Sumida River. Compare the temple grounds, nearby shopping streets and the density of surrounding buildings.

Kyoto — Kiyomizu-dera

Use Kiyomizu-dera as an anchor between the wooded hillside and the streets of Higashiyama. Look for the boundary between temple grounds, trees and the built-up city.

Yokohama — Minato Mirai

Follow the waterfront around Minato Mirai. Compare the outlines of docks, reclaimed land and present-day parks; the coastline is as useful a clue as the buildings.

Osaka Castle

Start with Osaka Castle and its moats. Compare the land outside the walls with the park and railway corridors visible on the current map.

Kamakura — Great Buddha

Locate the Great Buddha at Kotoku-in, then examine the temple grounds and roads towards Hase. Compare the wooded slopes with the neighbourhood below.

Kobe — Meriken Park

Use Meriken Park to explore Kobe’s harbour edge. Follow the piers and waterfront roads, looking for differences in the shape and use of the shoreline.

Okayama Castle

Follow the Asahi River around Okayama Castle. Compare the castle grounds and riverbanks before looking at the streets beyond them.

The Stone That Saved a Village

Locate the tsunami memorial stone and read the surrounding hillside on the map. This is a place to learn about local disaster memory; this page is not an evacuation map.

Nagasaki

Look for Nagasaki Peace Park in the valley. The selected historical layer is from the 1960s, so it shows the city after reconstruction had begun, not the immediate aftermath of 1945.

Sapporo

Find the grid of streets around central Sapporo. Follow a straight road across several blocks and compare open spaces, parks and the spread of buildings.

Hakodate

Compare Hakodate’s harbour with the streets rising towards the mountain. The narrow landform helps you keep your bearings as you change between photo layers.

Nagoya Castle

Use the moats of Nagoya Castle to align the two views. Compare the grounds inside them with the roads and city blocks outside.

Himeji Castle

Find Himeji Castle and follow the main approach south into the city. Compare the castle grounds, green spaces and the arrangement of streets around them.

Nara — Tōdai-ji

Locate Todai-ji and compare its grounds with the open areas of Nara Park. Use the transition from town to wooded hills to orient the photographs.

Kanazawa

Compare the castle area and Kenroku-en with the surrounding streets. Look for contrasts between broad grounds, narrow lanes and waterways.

Sendai

Look for the tree-lined avenues in central Sendai. Compare the width of streets and the distribution of green space across the city blocks.

Fukuoka — Hakata

Explore Hakata’s streets and waterways, then widen the view towards the waterfront. Compare the size of city blocks and the shape of the built-up area.

Naha — Shuri Castle

Compare the hilltop grounds of Shuri Castle with Naha’s surrounding streets. The selected 1970s imagery predates the 1992 reconstruction; check the official park website for current restoration and access information.
